About

I am a fourth-year PhD student in the SysNet group at the University of Toronto, advised by Niv Dayan in the ORCA Lab.

I design indexes and the data structures beneath them, and I hold each design to two standards. The first is theoretical: provable bounds on space and time. The second is practical: speed on real hardware, where cache lines, branch prediction, and memory bandwidth decide what is fast. My current focus is scalable vector indexes, which determine what semantic search costs in AI systems.

Outside research, I boulder and hike, read the poetry of Akhavan Sales and histories like Hobsbawm's The Age of Extremes, and play strategy games.

Projects

  • RisheریشهSource

    Persian and English share more ancestry than most people expect. Rishe maps it, using Ali Nourai's etymological dictionary. It lays out 950+ root families and 11,000+ words as a zoomable constellation, with searchable derivation trees, lines of classical poetry that show each word in use, and a pathfinder that connects any two words through their roots.

  • Philosophitor

    A study tool for philosophy built on the Philosophize This! transcripts. Given a philosopher or a question, such as free will or the basis of morality, it summarizes the main positions and then asks questions about the arguments themselves: what each one claims, the standard objections, and how the schools answer each other. The transcripts are searchable too.

  • pytse-client

    An open-source Python client for Tehran Stock Exchange data. I made price-history and order-book fetching fast and asynchronous, added a statistical analysis layer, and wrote the tests that kept it reliable as the exchange changed its interfaces.
